What this is

A ranch, in the cultural imagination, is vast — sprawling acreage, sweeping horizons, large-scale enterprise. Nanoranch reimagines that.

Here, a ranch is small scale. It's personal. It's the spaces we control and tend with intention: a backyard, a living room, a relationship, an internal landscape. It's quiet work, focused and unhurried, recognizing the grandeur that lives in the smallest of spaces and deepest within ourselves.

Nanoranch is a personal practice of intentional rewilding across three interconnected domains — the soil we tend, the spaces we inhabit, and the inner landscapes we cultivate.

It's rooted in the conviction that meaningful transformation happens at this scale — in backyards and living rooms, in relationships and daily choices — and that these acts of rewilding operate on a frequency that connects us to something larger than ourselves.

This practice is grounded in a consistent methodology: reflect, refract, and resonate. We look honestly at what's present, we imagine what could be, and we live into the results — learning as we go.

Resonance isn't just about outcomes. It's about finding the frequency that aligns with our deepest values and allows us to recognize the patterns, connections, and moments that matter most.

The three domains

Soil, space, and soul are not separate projects — they are nested expressions of the same practice, each tended at its own scale.

01

Soil

The literal ground we tend. Native plants, pollinators, host species, and the ecosystems we're calling back through intentional planting. This is where rewilding becomes visible and tangible.

02

Space

The physical environments we occupy and the relationships within them. How we design our homes, move through our yards, and show up for the people we love. Rewilding here means reclaiming authenticity in how we live — refusing manufactured convenience, and building spaces that reflect who we're actually becoming.

03

Soul

The internal landscape — our values, our grief, our growth, the people and moments that shape us. This is where we honor those we've lost, learn from those we love, and integrate the larger meaning of the work we're doing.

The three stages

Every rewilding effort moves through the same progression, operating on frequencies we're learning to recognize.

01

Reflect

We look. We inventory. We understand constraints and possibilities. Like light bouncing off a surface, reflection reveals what's actually present. We ask: what's here, and what do I need to understand about it?

02

Refract

We choose. We plan. We plant. We redesign. We practice. Like light bending through a prism, refraction breaks our vision into new angles and possibilities. We ask: given what I see, what am I called to do?

03

Resonate

We feel. We notice what moves us, what creates alignment throughout our entire being. Resonance is the frequency we feel vibrating through our core when something is true and connected — that full-body knowing, the tingle that tells us we're in harmony with what matters most.

Two places, one practice

Nanoranch takes root in two locations: Old Blue Fan Nanoranch in Austin, Texas, where this practice began, and Lori's Nanoranch (name TBD) in South Texas — an interpreter's sanctuary and gathering place that honors Lori Clay Malloy, whose gift for guiding others to see the living world shaped everything.

Lori's Nanoranch welcomes interpreters in residence — guides and naturalists carrying forward interpretive work. It welcomes creators — filmmakers and documentarians capturing the beauty of migration and habitat. And it welcomes artists — those translating wonder into form.

Both spaces are laboratories for small-scale rewilding, places where soil, space, and soul are being tended with intention.

Why this matters

This work is grounded in memory and gratitude. It honors Lori Clay Malloy, whose practice of finding wonder in the everyday continues to shape how we live. It's informed by the wisdom of our children and the relationships that sustain us.

And it's rooted in a simple belief: that reclaiming wildness — in our yards, our homes, and ourselves — is how we stay alive.

Small scale. Intentional. Honest. That's nanoranch.
